Now I am touching my screen. If I let go, which page will I end up on? The Home page or New Page?

![Inline Image Description]({{ 'assets/images/Tab bar on iOS/Apple_Music_New.jpg' | relative_url }})
The answer is New Page. Why? Because this thing I call the selection bubble is more visibly over New Page. Okay, simple, right?

What about now? Library, for sure, right?

![Inline Image Description]({{ 'assets/images/Tab bar on iOS/Apple_Music_Radio.jpg' | relative_url }})
No, Radio.

And if you think, ‘Hey, that doesn’t make sense,’ then that’s the right thought.

* * *

It all started in iOS 26, when Apple added the ability to switch between pages by dragging over the tab bar. It adds fluidity to movement across the app. That is cool. I have nothing against it.

The problem is simple. The selection bubble highlights everything under itself, no matter whether it is going to be selected. That is not a way to make predictable software. This UI element doesn’t clearly explain itself. The tab bar is one of the most used elements on the iPhone, and as a built-in element that appears across many apps, it could be better than this.

The sad part is that the iPadOS tab bar is better. There you can clearly see that the selection bubble highlights only the page that is going to be selected. It even highlights the part of the text that is not under the selection bubble. 

![Inline Image Description]({{ 'assets/images/Tab bar on iOS/iPad_Apple_Music.jpg' | relative_url }})

This explains itself much better than iOS does.

Why does iPadOS have this and not iOS? I think the iPad team realized it was a good idea because the selection bubble changes width to match the label or icon.

But what if I told you that the iOS tab bar changes width too? In the WhatsApp app, the Communities tab is wider than all the other tabs.
￼
![Inline Image Description]({{ 'assets/images/Tab bar on iOS/WhatsApp_Communities.jpg' | relative_url }})
![Inline Image Description]({{ 'assets/images/Tab bar on iOS/WhatsApp_Calls.jpg' | relative_url }})

So what about the selection bubble? The answer is that you can tell what is going to be selected, but not by the colour, only by the width.
￼
![Inline Image Description]({{ 'assets/images/Tab bar on iOS/WhatsApp_Calls_Hover.jpg' | relative_url }})

In the image below, it looks like the Calls tab is going to be selected, but in fact it is not. The bubble is too wide for the Calls tab. It is going to Communities.
￼
![Inline Image Description]({{ 'assets/images/Tab bar on iOS/WhatsApp_Communities_hover.jpg' | relative_url }})

Go ahead and try it.  Once you see it, you can’t unsee it.

I believe this could be a simple fix, if anyone inside Apple noticed it.
