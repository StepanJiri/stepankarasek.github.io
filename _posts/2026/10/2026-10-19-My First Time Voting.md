A week ago I voted for the first time. The voting process was very fast. This year in the Czech Republic there were local elections (komunální volby) and, in some regions, Senate elections. That was my case. I did not bring a ballot with me, so I got an empty one to fill out behind a screen. It was a large paper listing all the political parties (more than two parties, imagine that, America). The problem was that I had to fit the large paper into the envelope. That took me the most time, since I already knew how to vote.

In the first round of the Senate election, no candidate got more than 50 per cent, so there was another round the next week. Voting ran from Friday to Saturday until 2:00 PM. I was not at home that day, and I was sad that I would not be able to vote. But I managed to get to the local bus stop at 1:30 PM, and the next bus in the direction I wanted was in an hour and a half. At that moment I was pretty hopeless. But thanks to shared bikes, I was able to get to another part of the city and vote with 10 minutes to spare. The irony is that a week earlier I had tried shared bikes for the first time. My town pays for the first 15 minutes of the ride, and my ride took 15 minutes and 18 seconds. So it cost me around 1 dollar.

That reminded me of what Zohran Mamdani said. What I remember him saying was something like ‘I want to give my critics the option to take a bus and debate me.’

In the voting booth I was given a ballot to choose from again. I am 99% certain that I got only one ballot and one envelope by mistake. Logically, I was supposed to get at least two ballots. I did not tell the commission, because I got the ballot that I wanted.
